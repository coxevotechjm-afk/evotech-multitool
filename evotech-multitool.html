No safe size up to 4/0 AWG. No standard size up to 4/0 satisfies both the voltage-drop and ampacity requirements for this run. Do not use an undersized cable — use parallel runs or redesign the run (shorter cable, higher system voltage, or lower current) and check again.

The recommended size is the smallest standard AWG that satisfies both limits: its area keeps the voltage drop within your maximum, and its ampacity covers the current. Cable length is the one-way distance — total conductor length is twice that.

Optimal Panels Tilt - Based on your location

Tap a card to set it as your target — the live reading above highlights when you are within 2° of it.

Year-round figure: 18° — equal to your latitude, the fixed angle to use if the panels are not adjusted seasonally.

Practical minimum: in practice panels are rarely set below about 10° so rain can wash dust off them, even where the summer optimum is lower. At Jamaica’s latitude the summer optimum is 3°, so installers usually keep at least 10° rather than laying panels almost flat.
How the angles are worked out: year-round = latitude · summer = latitude − 15° (never below 0°) · winter = latitude + 15°. Based on latitude 18°.

Phone sensors are a guide, not a survey instrument. Check the final angle with the mounting hardware scale or an inclinometer before tightening down.

Panel Orientation

Align towards Equator

—
--°

Target: S · 180° — toward the equator

The green arc is the optimal band — 160° to 200°. Keep S at the red pointer at the top.

Tap the button above and allow motion / orientation access if your browser asks. Hold the phone flat, away from metal and magnets, and turn until S sits at the top pointer.

Calibration tip: if readings jump, move the phone in a figure-8 away from metal objects and electronics, then hold it flat and still.

Why south? Jamaica is in the northern hemisphere, so the sun’s path is mostly through the southern sky. Panels facing true south (180°) toward the equator catch the most sun over the year.
Magnetic vs true south: the phone compass reads magnetic heading, and Jamaica’s magnetic declination is small, so the reading is close enough inside the 160°–200° tolerance band. Keep the phone away from steel roofing, tools and the inverter while reading — metal pulls the compass off.

If the heading jumps around, wave the phone slowly in a figure-8 to recalibrate the compass, then read it again holding it flat and still.

How to use this on site

  1. Walk the house and tap Quick Add for each appliance you see, or type it in the custom form. Check the nameplate for the real wattage when you can.
  2. Use the kit’s typical hours — Quick Add already carries them (a fridge counts 24 hrs). Battery and inverter are sized on the critical loads only — the ones marked Critical — for 6 hours of backup.
  3. Tap Calculate Total for daily kWh and the recommended array, panels, battery and inverter.
  4. Send the summary to the office on WhatsApp or copy it into your site notes.

Defaults: 4.8 peak sun hours, 0.80 derate, 600W panels, 90% lithium depth of discharge, 6 hours of backup on critical loads, 25% inverter surge margin. You can adjust them in Settings for shaded or high-backup sites.
